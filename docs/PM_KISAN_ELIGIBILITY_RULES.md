# HAQDAAR — PM-KISAN Eligibility Rule Sheet

Version: 1.0  
Status: DRAFT — CURRENT VERIFICATION PENDING

## 1. Scheme coverage

- PM-KISAN is a Central Sector Scheme.
- Benefit: ₹6,000 per eligible farmer family per year.
- Payment: Three instalments of ₹2,000 each, every four months.
- Revised 2020 guidelines extend coverage to all eligible landholding farmer families, subject to exclusions.
- Do not apply the original two-hectare ceiling.

Source: Revised Operational Guidelines, 29 March 2020.

## 2. Family definition — Rule A

A farmer family consists of:
- Husband
- Wife
- Minor children

The family definition must not be expanded automatically to include parents, siblings or adult children.

## 3. Landholding requirement — Rule A

- The family must own cultivable land.
- Ownership must be established using applicable State/UT land records.
- State/UT land-record systems are used for beneficiary identification.
- Farmer status or occupation alone does not establish land ownership.
- Land belonging to another relative must not automatically be assigned to the speaker.

## 4. Exclusions — Rule A

Check whether any family member falls into the following categories:

1. Institutional landholders.
2. Former or present constitutional post holders.
3. Former or present Ministers and specified elected representatives.
4. Specified serving or retired Central/State Government employees and employees of covered organisations, excluding MTS/Class IV/Group D employees.
5. Retired pensioners receiving a monthly pension of ₹10,000 or more, subject to the specified employee-category exception.
6. Individuals who paid income tax in the last assessment year.
7. Registered professionals who actively practise as doctors, engineers, lawyers, chartered accountants or architects.

For government employment and pension questions, collect the person's category rather than relying on a simple yes/no answer.

## 5. Land ownership changes — Rule B

Historical clarification:

- Eligibility cutoff: 1 February 2019.
- Inheritance due to death is treated differently from ordinary land transfers.
- Transfers between 1 December 2018 and 31 January 2019 have historical proportionate-payment provisions.
- For post-cutoff inheritance, successor families may qualify if otherwise eligible.
- For post-cutoff sale, gift or partition, the transferee is not eligible under the historical clarification.
- The transferor's eligibility must be reassessed if the family no longer has cultivable land.

These provisions must be reconciled with the revised guidelines and current instructions before production use.

## 6. Joint holding — Rule B

Historical clarification:

- Joint holding refers to multiple members of the same defined family appearing in land records.
- The clarification identifies the family member with the highest quantum of landholding as the payment recipient.
- The original guidelines also describe an eldest-member tie-breaker when landholding quantities are equal.

Continued applicability of the historical interpretation and tie-breaker requires confirmation.

## 7. State-specific land provisions — Rule B

Manipur historical addendum:

- A Village Authority Chairman/Chief's certificate authorising cultivation of community-held land may be accepted.
- The certificate requires authentication by the concerned SDO.
- All scheme exclusions continue to apply.

Do not apply this provision to Rajasthan or other states.

## 8. Farmer information to collect — Rule C

- Speaker's name and age.
- State and district.
- Occupation.
- Whether cultivable land exists.
- Whose name appears in the land records.
- Land area and original spoken unit.
- Joint ownership details.
- Date and mode of acquisition.
- Whether ownership changed due to inheritance after death.
- Relevant family-member details.
- Government employment or pension category.
- Income-tax status.
- Professional registration and active practice.

Unknown answers must remain null.

## 9. HAQDAAR decision outcomes

1. Potentially eligible.
2. Potentially ineligible.
3. Needs verification.

The system must explain which conditions caused the result.

It must not:
- Treat unknown as false.
- Assume occupation means land ownership.
- Assign another relative's land to the speaker.
- Apply the outdated two-hectare limit.
- Treat a historical operational warning as an automatic exclusion.
- Invent land-unit conversions.
